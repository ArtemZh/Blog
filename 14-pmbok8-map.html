How to read the transition

The seventh edition is deliberately absent: it carried no processes at all, having replaced them with principles and performance domains. The eighth brings processes back, so the meaningful comparison runs from the sixth straight to the eighth — with the seventh sitting between them as the edition where the process layer was gone.

Three structural moves account for most of the shrinkage. Integration dissolved into Governance, which also took quality assurance and sourcing strategy, and which is the only domain with processes in all five focus areas. Cost widened into Finance — no longer only spend, but the financial viability of the deliverables. Communications stopped being an area of its own and moved wholesale into Stakeholders.

The rest is consolidation: four scheduling processes into one Develop Schedule, two risk analyses into one Perform Risk Analysis, two team processes into one Lead the Team. What looks like simplification is mostly the standard admitting these were never separate activities in practice.

Processes that stopped being processes

  • 8.1 Plan Quality Management — quality is no longer planned as its own thread; it carries as the principle Embed Quality Into Processes and Deliverables.
  • 8.3 Control Quality — absorbed into Manage Quality Assurance and Monitor and Control Project Performance.
  • 12.2 Conduct Procurements — absorbed into Acquire Resources; sourcing is planned once, in Governance.
  • 12.3 Control Procurements — absorbed into Monitor and Control Resourcing.
  • 6.2–6.4 Define Activities, Sequence Activities, Estimate Activity Durations — folded into a single Develop Schedule.
  • 11.3 / 11.4 Qualitative and Quantitative Risk Analysis — one Perform Risk Analysis, with both as techniques inside it.
  • 9.4 / 9.5 Develop Team, Manage Team — one Lead the Team.
