Processes Flow

The wall chart of the practice guide, redrawn for the eighth edition: forty processes instead of forty-nine, seven performance domains instead of ten knowledge areas, and the five process groups back under the name Focus Areas. Same sheet, same reading order — new contents.

The sheet

Colour carries one thing only: the domain a process belongs to. Dashed outlines group the processes of one domain; arrows run from a process to what it feeds. Click any process to isolate it and its links — click again, or press Escape, to release. Inputs and outputs live on the previous sheet.

⌘/Ctrl + wheel to zoom, drag to pan.

How this differs from the original chart

The practice-guide flow was drawn against the sixth edition's ten knowledge areas. Three of those rows no longer exist. Integration became Governance, which also absorbed quality assurance and sourcing strategy. Cost widened into Finance. Communications stopped being an area of its own and moved into Stakeholders; Procurement and Quality survive as single processes inside other domains rather than as columns.

The five containers are the part that did not move. Initiating, Planning, Executing, Monitoring and Controlling, Closing are still five, still in the same order — the eighth edition calls them Focus Areas and describes them as concepts a project can satisfy formally or informally, not as a mandatory sequence of gates.

Process names and their domains are facts from the standard; the numbering here is ours, following the practice guide's convention (4 Initiating, 5 Planning, 6 Executing, 7 Monitoring & Controlling, 8 Closing) — the eighth edition does not number its processes.
